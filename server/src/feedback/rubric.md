Score each answer from 1 to 4 on four things. Use the level whose sentence fits best.

Structure: does the answer have a clear order?
1. No order. Points jump around and there is no clear start or end.
2. Some order, but the main point comes late or is never stated.
3. A clear order with a beginning and an end, with one weak joint.
4. The point comes first, the steps follow in order, and it ends on the result.

Specificity: does the answer use real details?
1. Only general claims. No names, numbers or examples.
2. One or two details, but the key claims have no support.
3. Most claims have a concrete detail such as a number, a tool or an action.
4. Every key claim is backed by a concrete detail, and the candidate's own role is clear.

Relevance: does the answer address what was asked?
1. Mostly off the question.
2. Partly answers it and drifts for a good part of the time.
3. Answers the question with a small detour.
4. Stays on the question from start to end and every part helps the answer.

Concision: is the answer the right length for what it says?
1. Rambling or repeating, or so short that nothing is said.
2. Several sentences carry no information.
3. Mostly tight, with one stretch that could be cut.
4. Every sentence adds something and nothing needs cutting.
