# Sample bank: product designer, generic role

Invented questions for trying the interview room. No real company or person.

## Craft

### 1. "Walk me through a project you are proud of."

**Say:** "I led the redesign of a booking flow for a small travel app. Drop-off was high at the payment step. I interviewed eight users, cut the form from nine fields to four, and tested two prototypes. Completion rose from 54 to 71 percent."

**Follow-up:** What would you change if you did it again?

**Guard:** Do not claim the whole team result as mine alone.

### 2. "How do you decide what to design first?"

**Say:** "I start from the riskiest assumption. I list what must be true for the feature to work, rank by cost of being wrong, and design the top item first."

**Follow-up:** How do you handle a stakeholder who disagrees with the ranking?

**Guard:** Avoid the phrase "I just go with my gut".

### 3. "Tell me about a time you disagreed with an engineer."

**Say:** "An engineer wanted to drop a loading state to save a week. I showed session recordings where users tapped twice without it. We shipped a simple skeleton instead and kept the date."

**Follow-up:** What if the date had moved?

**Guard:** Do not blame the engineer.

### 4. "How do you work with a design system?"

**Say:** "I use the system first and extend it second. When a pattern is missing, I check three places it is already used, then propose it with usage notes."

**Follow-up:** Show me an example of an extension you proposed.

**Guard:** Do not say systems limit creativity.

## Research

### 5. "How do you run a quick usability test?"

**Say:** "Five people, one task each, a prototype that looks real. I watch for hesitation, not opinions. I write the top three problems the same day."

**Follow-up:** How do you pick the five people?

**Guard:** Never say five users is statistically valid.

### 6. "What do you do when you have no research budget?"

**Say:** "I use support tickets, a short survey to existing users, and two hallway-style calls. It is not perfect, and I say so in the readout."

**Follow-up:** What would you refuse to decide without research?

**Guard:** Do not downplay research as a luxury.

### 7. "Describe a time your research changed the plan."

**Say:** "A survey showed that most users never opened the filter panel we planned to rebuild. We moved the time to search results instead."

**Follow-up:** How did the team react?

**Guard:** Keep numbers to the ones in the readout.

## Collaboration

### 8. "How do you give design feedback?"

**Say:** ⚠️ No answer written yet. Draft one from the critique notes.

**Follow-up:** How do you give feedback to someone senior?

**Guard:** Do not say "I am direct" without an example.

### 9. "How do you handle a late scope change?"

**Say:** "I ask what problem the change solves, then show the cheapest version that solves it. I list what moves out to make room."

**Follow-up:** What if the change is not negotiable?

**Guard:** Do not agree to everything on the spot.

### 10. "What is your role in a sprint planning meeting?"

**Say:** "I bring designs that are ready for the next sprint and flag those that are not. I also raise open questions that would block build."

**Follow-up:** How early do you share unfinished work?

**Guard:** Avoid saying design is always ahead of engineering.

## Motivation

### 11. "Why this role?"

**Say:** "The product has a real onboarding problem, and I like fixing those. The team size also fits how I work, close to engineers and close to users."

**Follow-up:** What would you want to learn in the first month?

**Guard:** Do not criticise a past employer.

### 12. "Where do you want to be in three years?"

**Say:** "Leading design on one product area, mentoring one or two designers, and still doing hands-on work every week."

**Follow-up:** Would you move into management?

**Guard:** Do not promise to stay forever.
